# FIELD STATION

# MOTION_SYSTEM

**Version:** 1.0

**Status:** Active

**Repository:** field-station

---

> *Nothing moves without purpose.*

---

# Purpose

Motion in Field Station is not decoration.

It is communication.

Every movement should teach something about the natural world.

Visitors should feel as though they are standing inside a living ecosystem rather than interacting with software.

Motion exists to reinforce observation.

Never to distract from it.

---

# Motion Philosophy

Movement follows natural processes.

Everything has weight.

Everything has inertia.

Everything responds to time.

Nothing snaps.

Nothing teleports.

Nothing bounces.

Nothing spins for attention.

If motion cannot be explained by ecology or physics, it does not belong.

---

# Guiding Principles

Movement should feel:

• Quiet

• Deliberate

• Organic

• Predictable

• Calm

• Physical

The visitor should rarely notice animation itself.

They should notice what it communicates.

---

# Motion Hierarchy

Motion follows this priority.

1. Weather

2. Light

3. Ecology

4. Architecture

5. Objects

6. Interface

Nature always moves first.

The interface always moves last.

---

# Atmospheric Motion

Examples include:

Clouds

Fog

Mist

Dust

Rain

Snow (future)

Sunbeams

Moonlight

Cloud shadows

These systems move continuously.

Visitors should feel the passage of time even while standing still.

---

# Ecological Motion

Living systems never remain static.

Examples:

Prairie grasses

Oak branches

Leaves

Fern fronds

Moss moisture

Fungal growth (very slow)

Water

Insects

Birds

Everything behaves according to biological rhythms.

---

# Architectural Motion

Buildings also move.

Examples:

Doors swing naturally.

Curtains respond to breezes.

Window reflections shift.

Shadows move across floors.

Dust becomes visible in sunlight.

Nothing mechanical should feel robotic.

---

# Scientific Objects

Every object has its own behavior.

Notebook

Pages open slowly.

Compass

Needle settles naturally.

Maps

Fold and unfold.

Books

Open with weight.

Weather instruments

Update gently.

Microscope

Focus wheel rotates.

Telescope

Tracks celestial motion.

Objects should behave like their real-world counterparts.

---

# Interface Motion

Technology remains subtle.

Hover effects are inspired by nature.

Examples:

Morning dew

Opal

Thin-film interference

Soap bubbles

Butterfly wings

Interfaces should shimmer rather than glow.

The visitor should discover interactivity.

Never be instructed.

---

# Camera Motion

The visitor never teleports.

Movement resembles walking.

Acceleration is gentle.

Stopping is gradual.

Transitions between rooms feel physical.

The camera becomes another participant in the architecture.

---

---

# Sound Synchronization

Motion and sound should reinforce one another.

Wind moves grass.

Grass produces sound.

Rain strikes glass.

Clouds darken rooms.

Birds move before being heard.

Every movement should have consequence.

---

# Things We Never Animate

Buttons

Logos

Navigation

Headings

Decorative icons

Text for attention

Nothing moves merely to attract clicks.

---

# Motion Test

Before approving any animation ask:

Does it obey physics?

Does it support ecology?

Does it improve understanding?

Would this happen in the real world?

If the answer is no—

Remove it.

---

# Final Principle

The visitor should never think:

"That animation looks nice."

Instead they should think:

"The station feels alive."

That difference defines the motion language of Field Station.
