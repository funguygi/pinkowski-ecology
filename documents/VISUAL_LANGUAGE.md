# Purpose

This document defines the visual identity of Field Station.

Every illustration, interface, animation, room, object, and landscape should feel like it belongs to the same institution.

Visitors should recognize Field Station immediately without seeing its logo.

---

# Visual Identity

Field Station exists between four worlds.

• National Park research station

• Natural history museum

• Contemporary ecological laboratory

• Solarpunk architecture

It should never become:

• Corporate

• Futuristic

• Cyberpunk

• Minimalist tech

• Dashboard software

Technology is always secondary.

Nature is always primary.

---

# Architectural Character

The station feels handcrafted.

Warm.

Quiet.

Timeless.

Materials show age gracefully.

Objects reveal use.

Everything suggests ongoing scientific work.

# Color Philosophy

Color communicates ecology.

Morning:

Soft gold

Cool blue

Muted green

Spring:

Fresh greens

Wildflowers

Moist soil

Summer:

Golden grasses

Dry oak leaves

Deep shadows

Autumn:

Warm browns

Copper

Ochre

Winter:

Rain clouds

Emerald hills

Granite gray

Muted palettes are preferred.

Color should emerge naturally from materials.

---

# Lighting

Natural light defines every room.

Morning is the default experience.

Interior lighting remains warm.

Exterior lighting remains cool.

Sunlight should visibly travel through the building.

Night transforms the architecture.

Darkness is never feared.

It becomes another exhibit.

---

# Texture

Visitors should almost feel materials.

Wood grain.

Stone.

Paper fibers.

Pressed leaves.

Leather.

Canvas.

Moss.

Glass condensation.

The interface should feel tactile.

# Motion

Motion reflects physical processes.

Clouds drift.

Grass bends.

Dust floats.

Leaves fall.

Rain streaks windows.

Birds glide.

Nothing bounces.

Nothing spins.

Nothing flashes.

---

# Interaction

Curiosity is the interface.

Interactive objects invite attention.

They never demand it.

Hover effects resemble:

Morning dew

Opal

Thin-film interference

Soap bubbles

Butterfly wings

The interaction language belongs to biology.

---

# Typography

Typography should resemble museum exhibits.

Calm.

Readable.

Elegant.

Confident.

Quiet.

Never decorative.

---

# Composition

Every room follows the same hierarchy.

Landscape first.

Architecture second.

Objects third.

Interface last.

The visitor notices nature before software.

---

Wonder.

Stewardship.

Not distraction.

# Visual Test

Before approving any design ask:

Could this exist?

Would a field ecologist recognize this place?

Would a museum curator appreciate the craftsmanship?

Would a National Park ranger feel comfortable here?

Would someone want to stay longer?

If every answer is yes—

It belongs.
