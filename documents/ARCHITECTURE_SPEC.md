# 1. Architectural Intent

Field Station is designed as a permanent ecological research institution.

It is neither a house nor an office.

It is not a museum.

It is an active field station.

Visitors should immediately understand that scientific work happens here every day.

The architecture exists to cultivate observation.

Nothing in the building exists purely for decoration.

Every room supports curiosity.

Every transition teaches scale.

# 3. Building Organization

The building consists of five primary zones.

• Observation Deck

• Library

• Laboratory

• Collections

• Landscape

Every future room belongs within one of these systems.

---

# 4. Observation Deck

The Observation Deck serves as the orientation space for every expedition.

Visitors should immediately understand:

Where they are.

What today's conditions are.

Where they can explore.

The room is intentionally calm.

Architecture takes precedence over technology.

Primary elements include:

• Geodesic dome

• Observation window

• Living moss wall

• GIS wall

• Library alcove

• Research workbench

• Observatory stair

• Field door

---

# 5. Geodesic Observatory Dome

The dome is the defining architectural element.

Primary functions:

• Structural roof

• Skylight

• Passive ventilation

• Rainwater collection

• Astronomy platform

• Climate observatory

Construction:

• Laminated white oak ribs

• Steel connector hubs

• Triangular insulated glazing

• Integrated lighting

During daylight:

The dome frames sky, clouds, and weather.

At night:

The dome transforms into an observatory.

Visible systems include:

• Constellations

• Planet positions

• Moon phase

• Meteor showers

• Satellite tracking

• Light pollution

The dome is both shelter and scientific instrument.

---

# 18. Lighting Strategy

Morning serves as the default experience.

Interior lighting:

Warm.

3000K.

Exterior lighting:

Cool daylight.

Approximately 6000K.

Natural light remains the primary illumination.

Artificial lighting supports rather than dominates.
