# 1. Vision

Field Station is an interactive ecological research institution.

It is not a portfolio.

It is not a personal website.

It is not a collection of projects.

It is a place.

Visitors do not browse pages.

They inhabit spaces.

Research is presented as a living process rather than a collection of accomplishments.

The station exists to cultivate curiosity before communicating expertise.

Every room asks a scientific question.

Every object serves a scientific purpose.

Every transition follows the logic of ecological observation.

The website is never the subject.

Nature is.

---

# 2. Core Principles

Field Station is governed by the following principles.

• Place before Person.

• Questions before Conclusions.

• Observation before Interpretation.

• Scale is Navigation.

• Time is the Hidden Axis.

• Science is Visible.

• Interdisciplinary by Nature.

• Beauty Serves Understanding.

• Stewardship Through Knowledge.

• Every Discovery Leads to Another Question.

Nothing exists merely because it looks attractive.

Everything exists because it supports scientific observation.

---

# 3. Design Philosophy

Field Station answers one question.

**How does an ecologist observe the world?**

It does not answer:

"What projects has this scientist completed?"

Knowledge is discovered through exploration.

Visitors should feel like explorers rather than users.

The interface should quietly disappear.

Curiosity becomes navigation.

Wonder becomes interaction.

Learning becomes movement.

# 6. Observation Deck

The Observation Deck is the heart of Field Station.

Every expedition begins here.

The room immediately answers five questions.

Where am I?

What is happening today?

What landscape surrounds me?

What tools are available?

Where should I begin?

The Observation Deck should evoke:

• National Park research stations

• UC Natural Reserve facilities

• Botanical museums

• Mountain observatories

• Solarpunk ecological architecture

without imitating any specific institution.

The room should feel warm, intelligent, handcrafted, and quietly optimistic.

# 22. Design Rules

Before adding any feature, ask:

1. Where does it physically exist?

2. What scientific purpose does it serve?

3. Does it deepen curiosity?

4. Would a visiting ecologist believe it belongs here?

If the answer is no, redesign it.

# 24. The Field Station Oath

We build places that invite observation before explanation.

We make science tangible without sacrificing rigor.

We honor craftsmanship in ecology and software equally.

We design for curiosity rather than consumption.

We leave room for wonder.

Every decision should help someone become a more careful observer of the natural world.

Field Station is never finished.

Like ecology itself, it continues to grow.
